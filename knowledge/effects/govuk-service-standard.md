# GOV.UK Service Standard, the points about journeys

Source: <https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard>. Pulled 12 Oct 2026. Points
published 8 May 2019; point 1, 4 and 5 last updated 30 May 2022, point 3 on 16 Dec 2025,
point 2 on 29 Jan 2026.

From the GOV.UK Service Manual, Government Digital Service. Crown copyright, published under
the Open Government Licence v3.0. "The Service Standard helps teams to create and run great
public services." It has 14 points. Points 1 to 5 judge the journey and are summarised here,
with short quotes in GOV.UK's wording. Points 6 to 14 are about the team, technology and
operations and are listed by title only.

A finding cites the point by number and title, and states the consequence for the actor as
well.

## The points about the journey

| # | Point | GOV.UK's summary | Why it matters, in GOV.UK's words |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Understand users and their needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-1-understand-user-needs) | "Develop a deep understanding of users and the problem you're trying to solve for them." | "The real problem might not be the one you originally thought needed solving." Look at "the full context to understand what the user is trying to achieve, not just the part where they have to interact with government." |
| 2 | [Solve a whole problem for users](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-2-solve-a-whole-problem) | "Work towards creating a service that solves a whole problem for users, working with other teams and organisations where necessary." | "Users should not have to understand how government works in order to use public services." Services should be "scoped according to how users think and what they need to do - not too narrow or too broad." |
| 3 | [Provide a joined up experience across all channels](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-3-join-up-across-channels) | "Work towards creating a service that meets users' needs across all the channels they need. This could be a mix of online, phone, paper and face to face." | "Users should not be excluded or have an inferior experience because they lack access to technology or the skills to use it." |
| 4 | [Make the service simple to use](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use) | "Build a service that's simple, intuitive and comprehensible. And test it with users to make sure it works for them." | "People expect services to just work." Help the user do what they need "as simply as possible - so that people succeed first time, with the minimum of help", with "a consistent experience from start to finish." |
| 5 | [Make sure everyone can use the service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-5-make-sure-everyone-can-use-the-service) | "Provide a service that everyone can use, including disabled people and people with other legally protected characteristics." | "Inclusive, accessible services are better for everyone. For example, using simple words helps people who are in a hurry as well as people who have a learning disability." |

## The other points, by title

6. Have a multidisciplinary team
7. Use agile ways of working
8. Iterate and improve frequently
9. Create a secure service which protects users' privacy
10. Define what success looks like and publish performance data
11. Choose the right tools and technology
12. Make new source code open
13. Use and contribute to open standards, common components and patterns
14. Operate a reliable service
